# Researcher manual: dinner game prototype (first test)

For the researcher running the first test. The family only sees the phone app and a paper game board. You run the game itself.

## What the prototype is
- **Phone app** (web, English or Dutch): the children pick tonight's foods and read a fact about each one, read or listen to a short story, photograph their toy, and after dinner enter where the toy ended up.
- **Paper game board** (replaces the electronic tablecloth): a start square followed by 10 squares. The child's toy is the player piece.
- **You** (replace the tablecloth electronics and the LEDs): you pick who does the next task and which task it is, using the dice. The app does none of this.

## Before the test
- Open the app on the family's phone (Chrome on Android or Safari on iPhone both work). The camera needs permission.
- Choose the language (English or Nederlands) on the first screen. It can be changed later under **Grown-ups**.
- Have ready: the paper board, the dice, and the foods the family is eating.
- Nothing is uploaded. Everything, including the toy photo, stays on that phone.

## Step by step
| # | Who | What happens | Screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parent | Enter the child(ren)'s name(s), tap **Next**, then **We're ready!** | Hello, grown-up! |
| 2 | Children | Pick the foods that are on the plate tonight. Each pick shows a fact; tap **Put it on my plate**. When done, tap **That's my plate**. | What are you eating for dinner tonight? |
| 3 | Children | Check the plate. Tap a food to read about it again. Tap **Done! Read the story**. | Our dinner game |
| 4 | Children | Read the story, or press **Listen**. Tap **Next**. | Story time |
| 5 | Children | Take a photo of the toy (or choose a photo), pick an ink colour, give the toy a name. | Choose our toy |
| 6 | Children | Put the toy on the start square of the paper board. Eat and play. | Dinner time! |
| 7 | Children | After dinner tap **Dinner finished**, then pick the square the toy ended on. | Dinner finished! |

The toy is photographed only the first time. Later dinners start again at step 2 and skip step 5 until the toy has finished the track.

## Running the game at the table
There is one shared toy for all the children. It does not matter who moves it.

**The tasks** (shown as icons on the original tablecloth):
1. Smell a particular food
2. Touch a particular food
3. Take a bite
4. Steal a bite from someone else
5. Close your eyes. This is always combined with another task.

**Rules**
- The tasks can go to children and to parents.
- Doing the task moves the toy **one square forward**.
- Not doing the task means the toy **stays where it is**. It never moves back.
- Fill in how you use the dice here (who is picked, how the task is picked, how "close your eyes" is combined): _to be decided by the researcher before the test_.
- Never pressure a child to eat. The aim is curiosity: skipping a task is always fine.

## Finishing
1. After dinner, tap **Dinner finished** in the app.
2. Enter the square the toy is on (0 to 10). Square 10 is the end of the track.
3. If the toy reached square 10, tap **Put on our shelf**. It goes into the collection and the family can choose a new toy next time. The app then stays on the Shelf screen.
4. If not, the toy keeps its square and the game continues at the next dinner.

## Things to know about this prototype
- The paper board stands in for the tablecloth. There is no NFC and no position sensing, so you or the family must tell the app where the toy is.
- The food facts are general knowledge placeholders. The story text is a placeholder too.
- There is one menu and one toy for the whole family. The app does not know which child is using the phone.
- The phone remembers things between uses. To start clean, open **Grown-ups** and choose **Erase everything on this phone**.
- If the camera does not work, use **Use a photo instead** to pick a photo from the phone.

## Suggested notes during the test
- Which foods did the children pick, and did they pick unfamiliar ones?
- Did they read, listen to, or skip the story?
- Could they get through the app steps without help?
- Which tasks were done, skipped, or refused? Which foods were involved?
- What did the children say about the toy and the game?
